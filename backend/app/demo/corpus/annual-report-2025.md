# Northwind Renewables — Annual Report 2025

## Letter from the Chief Executive Officer

Dear shareholders,

Dana Whitfield became Chief Executive Officer in March 2021. Since then, the company
has more than doubled its installed generating capacity while holding to the
operating principles that have defined us since our founding: build close to the
communities we serve, contract revenue before we break ground, and report our
numbers the same way whether the year is easy or hard.

Revenue in 2025 was €412 million. This was up from €356 million in 2024. Total
installed capacity across our onshore wind, offshore wind, and solar portfolios
reached **1.8 GW** at year end, spread across nine countries in Northern and Western
Europe. We closed the year with 1,240 employees, a net addition of 94 roles, almost
all of them in project engineering and operations as we prepare to bring the Aurora
offshore project into construction.

Our largest single undertaking, the Aurora offshore wind farm, remains on schedule.
Turbine foundation fabrication began in the fourth quarter. Aurora is expected to
commission in the third quarter of 2027. Aurora will add 600 MW of firm, contracted
offshore capacity off the coast of our largest operating market and is fully
underwritten by a 20-year power purchase agreement signed in 2023.

On the environmental side of our business, we again cut the carbon intensity of the
electricity we generate. Emissions intensity was 34% below the 2020 baseline. This
was driven by the retirement of two older gas-backed balancing contracts and a full
year of operation from the Kestrel Ridge onshore wind expansion. We are proud of
that trajectory and know it must continue every year, not just in years when the
wind cooperates.

Sincerely,
Dana Whitfield
Chief Executive Officer

## Financial Highlights

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | €412M | €356M | +15.7% |
| EBITDA | €187M | €159M | +17.6% |
| Installed capacity | 1.8 GW | 1.5 GW | +0.3 GW |
| Employees | 1,240 | 1,146 | +94 |
| Emissions intensity vs. 2020 | −34% | −27% | −7 pts |

Revenue growth was driven by a full year of contribution from the Kestrel Ridge
onshore expansion (commissioned March 2024) and higher merchant power prices in our
Nordic solar assets during the second and third quarters. EBITDA margin held steady
at 45%, consistent with the prior year, as higher revenue was partially offset by
increased development spend ahead of Aurora's construction phase.

## Operations Review

The onshore wind segment contributed 61% of generation, offshore wind (pre-Aurora,
from our existing Meridian array) contributed 22%, and solar contributed the
remaining 17%. Availability across the fleet averaged 97.1%, in line with our
five-year average and slightly ahead of the 96.8% recorded in 2024.

Construction activity was concentrated on two fronts this year. First, early works
for Aurora — seabed surveys, onshore substation groundworks, and turbine foundation
fabrication at our contracted supplier's yard — proceeded without material delay.
Second, we began repowering the Fjellheim onshore site, replacing 40 aging turbines
with 22 higher-capacity units for a net capacity gain of 35 MW, expected to complete
in 2026.

We ended the year with a project pipeline of twelve developments at various stages
from early permitting to construction, described in full in our project pipeline
disclosure. Combined, these represent a further 2.4 GW of potential capacity should
all reach financial close.

## Sustainability

Beyond the emissions intensity figure above, we tightened our supplier code of
conduct, requiring all turbine and balance-of-plant suppliers above €5M in annual
spend to report Scope 3 emissions from 2026 onward. Water use per MWh generated fell
9%, and we recorded zero reportable environmental incidents for the third consecutive
year.

Employee safety also improved: our total recordable incident rate fell to 1.8 per
200,000 hours worked, down from 2.3 in 2024, reflecting the safety programme rolled
out across all construction sites in the second half of 2024.

## Outlook for 2026

We expect revenue growth to moderate slightly in 2026 as merchant price tailwinds
normalise, before accelerating again in 2027 as Aurora approaches commissioning. Group
capital expenditure guidance for 2026 is €640–680M, weighted toward Aurora
construction and the Fjellheim repowering programme. We remain confident in our
ability to fund this growth from a combination of operating cash flow, our existing
green bond programme, and non-recourse project financing at the asset level.

We thank our employees, communities, and shareholders for their continued support and
look forward to reporting further progress in 2026.
