# Board Memorandum: Aurora Offshore Wind Project Update

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** Tomas Vávra, Chief Development Officer
**Date:** 8 December 2025
**Re:** Aurora offshore wind project — schedule, budget, and risk update

## Purpose

This memorandum updates the board on the status of the Aurora offshore wind project
ahead of the December board meeting. It supersedes the schedule and budget figures
circulated in the Q3 project update.

## Schedule

Since the Q3 update, the project team has identified a cumulative four-month delay
to the construction schedule, driven primarily by the fabrication and risk items
described below. Aurora is now expected to commission in the first quarter of 2028.
This is revised from the third quarter of 2027 previously communicated externally.
Management recommends that any external communication continue to reference the
prior commissioning date until the revised schedule has been fully validated with
the balance-of-plant and installation contractors, expected by the February board
meeting.

The revised critical path runs through monopile fabrication, offshore installation
vessel availability, and grid connection works at the onshore substation. Turbine
foundation fabrication, which began in Q4 2025 as reported externally, is proceeding
but at a slower rate than the fabrication yard's original commitment.

## Budget

Total project capital expenditure is now estimated at **€1.1 billion**, compared
with the €980 million sanctioned at final investment decision in 2023. The increase
is driven by the schedule slippage described above (approximately €70 million of
carrying and extended-overhead costs) and a revised steel price assumption for the
remaining monopile fabrication scope (approximately €50 million). The project
remains within the contingency envelope approved at final investment decision, and
management does not currently expect to seek additional board approval for the
revised budget, though this will be kept under review.

## Key Risks

1. **Fabrication yard capacity.** The contracted fabrication yard is running at
   reduced throughput due to a separate customer's project also occupying yard
   capacity. Management is evaluating a secondary fabrication source for the
   remaining monopiles not yet started.

2. **Installation vessel availability.** The specialised heavy-lift installation
   vessel booked for Aurora has a narrow window in 2027 shared with two other
   projects in the region. A schedule slip on either of those projects could
   further delay Aurora's installation campaign.

3. **Permitting for the onshore cable route.** A permitting objection from a
   landowner along the onshore cable route was resolved in November 2025, but a
   similar objection from an adjacent landowner has been lodged and is under
   review by the relevant authority.

4. **Steel and copper price volatility.** Approximately 40% of the remaining
   procurement scope is not yet price-locked, exposing the project to further
   commodity price movements.

5. **Grid connection works.** Onshore substation groundworks are complete, but the
   transmission system operator's own upgrade works at the point of connection are
   tracking two months behind the operator's original schedule, which could become
   the binding constraint on commissioning if not resolved.

## Mitigations Underway

The project team has engaged a secondary fabrication yard for preliminary
qualification, is in commercial discussions to secure a backup installation vessel
slot in early 2028, and has price-locked an additional 15% of remaining procurement
scope since the Q3 update. Management will provide a full mitigation plan at the
February board meeting alongside the validated revised schedule.

## Recommendation

The board is asked to note the revised schedule and budget, and to approve
continued engagement with a secondary fabrication yard, which requires
qualification spend of up to €4 million against the existing contingency.
